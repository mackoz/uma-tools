Sim vs. Reality

How close does the simulator get to a real race?

We took real Champions Meeting races, fed the same horses and skills into the simulator, and compared what actually happened to what the simulator predicted. Here's what we found — in plain terms first, numbers if you want them.

So, is it accurate?

Short answer: pretty close, but we can't yet prove it's close enough to trust the exact skill-comparison numbers umalator gives you.
The simple version

We looked at this two ways. First: if you pick one specific real race and ask the simulator to run it, how far off does it land? Second, and more important: when umalator tells you "this skill is worth about half a horse-length," can we actually trust that number? Those turn out to be different questions with different answers.

—

One important catch: to really prove "does this skill help by exactly this much," you'd want to run the same horse twice — once with a skill, once without — and compare. Every horse in this data set only raced with one skillset, never a with/without pair. So we can't directly confirm umalator's exact per-skill numbers here, only get a rough sense of how far off the simulator tends to be in general.

What throws the simulator off?

—
The simple version

Two things: how often a horse's running style tends to get bigger errors, and whether it got boxed in (blocked) during the race. Neither is shocking — traffic is genuinely hard to simulate, and front-runners live or die by the pace in ways that are sensitive to small differences.

Did the simulator predict the right finishing order?

Somewhat — better than a coin flip, not perfect. 1.0 would mean it nailed the order every time; 0 would mean it was just guessing.
